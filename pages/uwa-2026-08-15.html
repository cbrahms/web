Claims this week's data supports

  • AI-capex fragility. The BIS named it in June; in July a fund carrying 4× leverage against SanDisk and Micron proved the transmission mechanism in three weeks.

Net effect. The composite barely moved — 7.2 to 7.4 — but its internals rotated substantially. What this dashboard was reading as elite anticipation now looks more like sovereign arithmetic plus a levered accident. That is a less cinematic story and a better-evidenced one.

The US paid the most for thirty-year money since 2001 — and the auction still tailed

Treasury data

On 13 August the Treasury sold $25B of bonds maturing August 2056 at a high yield of 5.216%, against a median bid of 5.150% — a tail, meaning the auction cleared worse than the middle of the book. Bid-to-cover slipped to 2.39 from 2.44 at the July reopening. Indirect bidders, largely foreign central banks and international accounts, took 66.8% of competitive awards; dealers were left with only 11.5%. The 10-year closed the prior session at 4.68% and the 2-year at 4.20%, so the curve is steepening from the long end rather than flattening from the short end. Interest expense on the public debt has reached $1.170 trillion through July of fiscal 2026 against total debt of $39.91 trillion.

High yield 5.216% Median bid 5.150% — tailed Bid-to-cover 2.39 (was 2.44) Indirects 66.8% Dealers 11.5% Avg rate on all debt 3.447% FY26 interest $1.170T
Why it is significant: this is not a failed auction — foreign demand turned up in size. The signal is the price they demanded. Buyers are charging term premium for duration risk in a currency whose central bank's reaction function they no longer feel they can model, and they are doing it while equity volatility sits at 14.63. Note also the composition: dealers took the smallest slice, which argues the clearing level was genuinely market-set rather than absorbed by obligation. The arithmetic is the story — at a 3.447% average coupon across $39.9T, every dollar refinanced at 5.216% adds 177bp to the interest bill, and Treasury has been shortening issuance to avoid exactly that.
Sources: Prime Rates auction breakdown · Bloomberg — "Costliest US Bond Sale Since 2001 Is Investor Warning to Bessent" · Advisor Perspectives · Treasury quarterly refunding statement

A $45B AI fund liquidated in three weeks, and the tape barely registered it

13F + reporting

Situational Awareness LP, run by 24-year-old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, lost roughly 67% in July and sold its entire $16B public equity portfolio to Citadel on 30 July at a discount of about 10%, to meet margin calls from Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Bank of America. The fund had run about 4× leverage into concentrated AI-hardware longs and had returned 439% through June, growing from $225M at inception to a peak near $45B. Its Q2 13F, filed 14 August, is a photograph of the portfolio the day before it went: $20.2B disclosed, SanDisk at 28% and Micron at 27.5% — 55% in two names — with the top ten at 92%. Citadel's Wellington fund gained 5.9% in July on the other side of the trade.

−67% in July $45B → ~$10B AUM $16B book sold 30 Jul ~10% discount ~4× leverage Top 2 = 55% of 13F Citadel Wellington +5.9%
Why it matters, and what it is not: it is not foreknowledge — nobody positioned ahead of this; the fund was the position. What it is, is the AI-capex fragility the BIS named in June, demonstrated end-to-end in under a month: concentration, prime-broker leverage, a forced seller, and a single buyer setting the price. The broader read is worse than the single name — JPMorgan puts tech-focused hedge funds down 10.2% in July, their worst month on record, with global equity long/short at its second-worst month in four years and industry deleveraging the most pronounced since 2022. A stress event of that size passed through with the VIX under 15, which tells you how little the headline volatility gauge is now measuring.

Gold has quietly passed Treasuries as the world's largest reserve asset

Official-sector data

Foreign central-bank gold holdings are worth roughly $4.0 trillion against approximately $3.9 trillion of US Treasuries — the first time gold has been the larger of the two since 1996. Gold's share of global central-bank reserves rose to 27% at the end of 2025 from 20% a year earlier, while the Treasury share fell to 22% from 25%. Q2 2026 official buying hit a record 289 tonnes, up 62% year on year, during gold's steepest quarterly decline in a decade; Q1 was 244 tonnes and the highest single-quarter dollar value on record at $37B. Of 76 central banks surveyed, 89% expect their gold reserves to rise over the next twelve months and 74% expect lower dollar holdings over five years. The largest additions since 2022 have come from China, Poland, Turkey and India. Spot gold has since recovered above $4,400.

Gold ~$4.0T vs USTs ~$3.9T Gold 20% → 27% of reserves USTs 25% → 22% Q2 buying 289t · +62% YoY 89% of 76 CBs to add First crossover since 1996
Why it is unusual: buying more as the price falls is the signature of a mandate rather than a trade, and the crossover converts a five-year de-dollarisation talking point into a measured fact. It is also the one thread on this page with a clean, stated causal driver — reserve managers repeatedly cite the 2022 freezing of Russian reserves — which makes it insurance against a monetary event rather than a bet on one. Read against the 30-year auction above, the two datasets describe the same thing from opposite ends: the price the world now charges to hold US duration, and what it is holding instead.
Sources: Mining.com on the ECB finding · Yahoo Finance · Q2 record tonnage via WGC data · Crux Investor on the survey

Households are rolling over while the index sits at a record

Survey data

The University of Michigan's preliminary August sentiment index came in at 51.0 against a 54.5 consensus, down from 55.2 in July — an 8% monthly fall that ends two months of recovery. Expected business conditions dropped 11% short-run and 17% long-run. The series all-time low of 44.8 was set in May 2026; August is a rebound from that floor, not a new bottom, but the direction has turned again and the level sits far below August 2025's 58.2. The S&P 500 closed the same session at 7,785.76, a fraction off its record, with VIX at 14.63 and a third consecutive weekly gain.

UMich 51.0 vs 54.5 exp. −8% MoM · from 55.2 Expectations −11% / −17% Series low 44.8 (May 2026) S&P 7,785.76 · VIX 14.63
Why it stays on the board: this is not an elite-behaviour signal and it is not evidence of foreknowledge. It is the denominator. An empire-in-decline reading that only tracks what the wealthy are doing will miss the condition that makes their behaviour consequential — an index at a record and a population at 51 is a legitimacy gap, and legitimacy gaps are what convert financial stress into political events. Weigh it accordingly: sentiment surveys have been poor predictors of consumer spending for four years running.

Hormuz remains closed in month six, and the ceasefire that was meant to reopen it has already collapsed

Continuing

Shipping through the Strait ran at 17% of the pre-conflict average over the past seven days — 151 vessels in or out. There have been 56 reports of damage to ships in and around the Strait since the war began on 28 February. The UAE reported Iran attacking two of its vessels, one on the evening of 13 August. A replacement carrier group is en route to relieve the USS Lincoln. The 8 April Pakistan-mediated ceasefire, which explicitly required reopening the Strait, collapsed on 8 July after mutual violations; the Islamabad Memorandum signed on 17 June set a 60-day window to reach final terms. The EIA's August outlook has Brent averaging about $85 in Q3, with US LNG exports revised down to 16.5 Bcf/d.

Why it is on the page at all: it is the largest single gap between what is happening and what the volatility surface is pricing. Roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil and LNG moves through a waterway that is operating at a sixth of capacity, in an active shooting war, and index volatility is at a yearly low. That is not a claim that anyone has inside knowledge; it is an observation that the market has decided to treat a live chokepoint closure as background noise, which is the sort of decision that reverses without warning.

Method and limits

Read this before acting on any of it

What this sweep looks for. Asset liquidation and repositioning, unusual money movement, property and jurisdiction purchases, relocation flows, coordinated behaviour among the ultra-wealthy, and cryptic or ominous public statements from people in a position to know something. Sources are weighted toward regulatory filings, auction results, official-sector datasets and surveys with published methodologies, and away from single-source claims, aggregator Substacks and anything traceable to one person's public posting.

What was searched and found nothing. No credible instance surfaced this window of a wealthy individual or institution making a specific, dated, non-public claim about a coming event. The phrases this sweep monitors for — "on good authority", "sworn to secrecy", "a little birdie", "something big is coming" — returned only recycled 2024–25 bunker content, the two debunked theories above, and general macro warnings already public since February. Absence of a signal is the finding, and it is reported as such.

Known weaknesses. 13F data is long-only, US-equity-only and six weeks stale, and excludes shorts, options and foreign listings — the single biggest blind spot on this page. Insider selling is routinely driven by pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans and is a poor market timer. The J.P. Morgan family-office survey cited as a counterweight was fielded mid-2025 and may no longer describe current behaviour. Sentiment surveys have decoupled from actual spending for four years. The panic meter is a subjective composite with no predictive record; its value is in tracking changes in its own components across entries, which is why this entry shows the deltas rather than just the levels.

Standing conclusion, five entries in. A large, well-documented divergence between elite positioning and retail positioning existed in H1 2026 and has partially closed in the Q2 filings. What remains is a set of structural stresses — sovereign borrowing cost, concentration in the AI complex, household condition, a closed chokepoint — that are individually public and collectively under-assembled. None of it requires or evidences foreknowledge of a specific event.
