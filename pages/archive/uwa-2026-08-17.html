Unusual Wealth Activity Page 001 Scan window: 17 Aug 2026 Pattern found

Everyone is heading for the exit at once, and nobody is being quiet about it

A scan for evidence that wealthy individuals and institutions hold private knowledge of an undisclosed coming event. What it found instead was a very large, very public, very well-documented defensive crouch.

Assessment

No signal in this scan supports the presence of secret foreknowledge. Every large repositioning found is disclosed, regulator-filed, or survey-reported — and the risks being hedged against are the ones already on the front page.

The finding that is real is the breadth: central banks, family offices, corporate insiders, Berkshire, and private-credit investors are all reducing risk simultaneously, and several of those channels are at record or near-record levels in their own histories. That is a coordination worth watching. It is not a conspiracy — it is the same public information being read the same way by many people at once.

AInsider flow

Executives are selling at the second-fastest pace in more than twenty years

Corporate insiders sold $77.6 billion of stock in the first half of 2026, up 20% year over year, per EPFR Global Market Intelligence. Only 2021 — peak stimulus — was more intense. August has continued the run: CoreWeave's CEO sold ~$27.4M on 11 August; DST Global sold ~$33.4M of Chime across 11–12 August.

Why it is unusual: the people with the most complete internal picture of their own companies are converting equity to cash at a rate seen once in two decades, and doing it into a market that has not broken.

Discount forMuch of this is pre-scheduled 10b5-1 selling and is concentrated in AI-linked names whose share prices are simply very high. Aggregate insider selling has a poor record as a market-timing signal on its own.

ADry powder

Berkshire is sitting on a record $397 billion and will not deploy it

As of 31 March 2026, Berkshire held a record $397B in cash and short-term Treasuries, past the previous $373B high. It sold $24.09B of equities in Q1 and bought only $15.94B — extending a net-selling streak past three years. The pile earns roughly $12B a year in interest at ~3.7% bill yields.

Why it is unusual: this is the largest corporate cash position ever assembled, held by the investor most publicly committed to being fully invested, sustained across three years rather than a quarter.

Discount forBerkshire's size limits its opportunity set — few acquisitions are big enough to matter. Bill yields near 3.7% make waiting cheap. Buffett has declined to attribute the cash to any market forecast.

AReserves

Central banks bought a record 289 tonnes of gold into the steepest price drop in a decade

Q2 2026 set a record for official-sector gold buying at 289 tonnes, following 244 tonnes in Q1 — and it happened while gold posted its worst quarterly decline in ten years. The World Gold Council's 2026 survey has 45% of central banks planning to add more in the next twelve months, up from 43% in 2025 and 29% two years ago. 74% expect the dollar's reserve share to fall over five years.

Why it is unusual: buying into a falling market is the tell. Price-insensitive accumulation means the buyer is not trading gold, it is reallocating away from something else — and the something else is dollar-denominated reserves that can be frozen by political decision, as Russia's ~$300B was in 2022.

Discount forThis trend is four years old and averages ~1,000 tonnes a year. It is a slow structural shift, not a new alarm. Note also that much of the loudest commentary on it is published by bullion dealers.

AAllocation

Family offices are rewriting allocations at the highest rate ever recorded

UBS surveyed 307 family offices averaging $2.7B net worth. Sixty percent plan to change strategic asset allocation in the next twelve months — the largest share in the survey's history. Among those making active changes, real estate falls from ~11% to 8% and private equity from ~21% to 18%, rotating into private credit, hedge funds, secondaries, infrastructure, and more gold. Major geopolitical conflict is the top-ranked concern over both one- and five-year horizons.

Why it is unusual: the direction of travel is toward shorter duration and faster exits — from illiquid ten-year commitments into things that can be sold. A record share of the world's largest private pools shortening duration at the same moment is a coordination signal even without any coordination.

Discount for"Plan to change allocation" is intent, not action, and survey wording shifts year to year. Some of the rotation is a straightforward response to real estate marks and PE distribution droughts, not to geopolitics.

ALiquidity

The four biggest private-credit managers are all limiting withdrawals

Blackstone gated redemptions on BCRED, its $79B flagship, in June 2026. Apollo, Ares, and Blue Owl are all capping withdrawals too. Investors requested more than $20.8B from the largest semi-liquid private-credit funds in Q1; managers met barely half. Across the BDC universe, $13.9B was requested and $4.6B gated. S&P Global projects AI-linked debt coming due jumps from $56.6B in 2026 to $215B in 2028.

Why it is unusual: this is the one signal where sophisticated money is visibly trying to leave and being told it cannot. Gates are contractual and orderly — but they convert a liquidity preference into a queue, and queues are where confidence gaps become price gaps.

Discount forGates are a designed feature of these vehicles, not a default event, and they are working as intended. Golub's July window saw requests fall to 4.8% and honored 100% of them — the pressure is uneven, not universal.

AMobility

Wealthy Americans are buying New Zealand residency at roughly six times the prior rate

More than 700 wealthy foreign nationals applied for New Zealand's golden visa in fourteen months, against 115 in the preceding three years. Of 1,833 total applicants, about 617 — nearly 40% — are wealthy Americans, with Californians heavily represented. Separately, Henley projects roughly 165,000 millionaire relocations globally in 2026 after a record ~142,000 in 2025, with the UK losing a projected 16,500.

Why it is unusual: residency is a slow, expensive, deliberate purchase. A six-fold jump concentrated in one nationality is a durable statement about where people expect to want to be.

Discount forNew Zealand cut the investment floor from $15M to $5M in April 2025, dropped the English-language requirement, and reduced required days in-country. A large share of the surge is simply a cheaper product finding its market. Henley has also stopped publishing precise counts and changed methodology after criticism.

BFragility

A $45 billion fund became a $10 billion fund in about a month

Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness returned 439% through June on concentrated, ~4× levered long positions in AI hardware — SK Hynix, Nebius, Micron. When semis fell in late July, prime brokers at Goldman, JPMorgan, and Bank of America issued margin calls. On 30 July the fund sold its entire public book, longs and shorts, to Citadel in a single pre-open block at roughly a 10% discount — about $16B, one of the largest rushed equity transactions in Wall Street history. The fund lost ~67% in July.

Why it matters, and why it is not what it looks like: this is a forced liquidation — the precise opposite of acting on foreknowledge. Its signal value is structural: it shows how fast a concentrated, levered AI book unwinds, and that a single buyer was able to absorb $16B at a 10% discount in one trade.

Discount forNothing here implies Citadel knew anything. Buying a distressed block at a discount is the normal business of a large market maker, and the trade was reported openly within a day.

BDisclosure gap

Treasury's own analysts wrote an AI-bubble warning the department will not publish

NOTUS obtained a completed internal Treasury draft, prepared for Secretary Bessent, Fed Chair Warsh, and financial regulators, finding AI firms "more deeply entrenched in the U.S. economy than their dotcom predecessors" and warning that a downturn would hit stock markets, private credit, data-center financiers, cloud providers, chipmakers, and utilities. It has sat completed for weeks awaiting approval. Treasury called the findings "unvetted" and restated that AI "will be a key driver of America's new Golden Age" — while Bessent publicly praised "$750 billion in AI buildout this year."

Why it is unusual: this is the closest thing the scan found to an information asymmetry — a documented divergence between what a government's analysts assess privately and what it says publicly. Anyone reading NOTUS on 6 July knew something the market's official messaging was not saying.

Discount forA draft staff paper is not policy, and dissenting internal analysis is routine and healthy. It has now been public for six weeks. It is a candid assessment, not a secret.

BPlumbing

The bad AI loans may end up in life insurers, backstopped by state guaranty funds

Private-credit firms are key owners of AI-related assets, and many of their private-equity parents also own life insurers — which can serve as destinations for loans that would be hard to place elsewhere, and which are ultimately backed by state guaranty funds. Roughly 34% of global fund managers now name AI hyperscaler spending as the most likely source of the next systemic credit event. Circular financing is visible in the open: Nvidia has guaranteed it will buy capacity CoreWeave cannot sell through 2032.

Why it is unusual: this is the specific transmission path from an AI correction to ordinary households, and it is discussed in trade and policy press while mainstream coverage stays on valuations and chip demand.

Discount forThe strongest version of this argument comes from an outlet with an explicit editorial position on private equity. The mechanism is plausible and partly documented; the scale of insurer exposure is contested and not established here.

CRecycled

The billionaire-bunker story contains no 2026 information

Current bunker coverage is built almost entirely from old material: Thiel's New Zealand citizenship (2011), Hoffman's "apocalypse insurance" quote to The New Yorker (2017), Vivos's 300-person South Island shelter (2020), Rising S's roughly ten NZ deliveries "over the past few years," Zuckerberg's Kauai land (2014–2025). Nothing in the current wave reports new construction, new permits, or new purchases dated 2026.

Why it is being flagged as noise: this material recirculates on an annual cycle with fresh headlines and unchanged facts. Treating it as a current signal is how a monitoring routine manufactures a pattern that is not there.

CNon-event

Bitcoin whale wake-ups are running at half of last year's volume

A dormant wallet moved 16,400 BTC (~$1.04B) on 3 August after seven months idle; a 12.5-year-dormant wallet moved 26.96 BTC on 10 August. Both went to new wallets, not exchanges. Galaxy Research reports the "great redistribution" of old bitcoin is largely complete and that 2026's woken-coin volume is less than half of last year's.

Why it is being flagged as noise: the aggregate trend is down. Individual wake-ups get headlines because they are legible, not because they are informative — custody upgrades, estate planning, and rebalancing are indistinguishable from exit on-chain.

What would change this assessment

Falsifiers

Stated in advance so the next scan can check them rather than reinterpret the same evidence. Any one of these would be materially harder to explain with public information alone.

  1. Concentrated, unhedged put buying or tail-risk positioning in a sector with no news flow attached to it.
  2. Simultaneous 13F exits by unrelated managers into the same non-obvious names, with no shared thesis published anywhere.
  3. Insider selling rotating out of AI names and into defensives, staples, or utilities — a shift in composition, not just volume.
  4. Physical-asset or land purchases clustering in one specific geography with no tax, residency, or policy change to explain it.
  5. New shelter or hardened-facility construction permits filed in 2026, as opposed to features recycling pre-2025 reporting.
  6. Officials, executives, or their family trusts liquidating ahead of a disclosure that later proves material — the pattern that actually constitutes foreknowledge.

How this page was built

Open-web search across wire services, financial trade press, SEC filings, official surveys, and niche outlets, conducted 17 August 2026. Everything here is public. No private forums, paid data, or non-public communications were accessed.

What was deliberately excluded: promotional content from bullion dealers and precious-metals sellers, which supplies a large share of de-dollarization commentary and has a direct financial interest in the conclusion; prediction-market price moves, which Bloomberg has documented as vulnerable to genuine insider trading; and political statements aimed at moving specific securities.

The central limitation: a scan of public sources cannot, even in principle, detect private foreknowledge. It can only detect the footprints of private foreknowledge — unexplained positioning, movement without a public reason. This scan found no such footprints. It found a great deal of well-explained positioning, which is a different thing and should not be reported as the same thing.

On the panic meter: the indicator scores are editorial judgments anchored to published measurements. Two careful analysts would score these differently by a point or two. The composite is a mean with no weighting and should be treated as a summary of the nine rows, not as a measurement in its own right.
