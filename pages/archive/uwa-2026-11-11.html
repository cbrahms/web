Assessment

Fact. Indirect bidders, the channel for foreign official and overseas buyers, took the smallest share of a 10-year auction in at least a year. That came on the day inflation beat forecasts for the second month running. The largest planned IPO cut its maximum raise by about $5B from the terms circulated a day earlier. Stock volatility and credit spreads fell back to pre-5 Nov levels.

Inference. Pressure moved from the equity desks to the Treasury market. Foreign buyers stepped back at a 5% coupon while the Fed signals more hikes. A US-only bid absorbing long debt at rising yields raises the cost of every refinancing, including AI build-out debt. The equity rebound has not been confirmed by any holder with the most capital at stake.

Not claimed. That foreign holders are selling Treasuries; one auction shows lower participation, not sales. That the IPO will price below range. Coordination among actors.

Foreign-channel bid drops at the 10-year auction

What
10 Nov: $42B 10-year notes, high yield 5.020%, about 1 bp above when-issued. Bid-to-cover 2.46 (2.77 in October; lowest since May). Indirect bidders 61.2% (80.3% in October). Direct bidders 29%. Primary dealers 9.8% (2.5%). Coupon 5%, highest since 2006. One report calls the 19.1-point indirect drop the largest auction-to-auction fall since July 2016.
Why unusual
Indirect bids carry foreign central-bank and overseas demand. The share fell by a quarter in one month, at the highest coupon in 20 years. Dealers were left with four times October's allocation.
Conventional read
Indirect share swings auction to auction; October's 80.3% was unusually high. A 1 bp tail on CPI day is modest. Domestic directs filled the gap.
