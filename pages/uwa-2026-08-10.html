Unusual Wealth Activity

Scan of 2026-08-10 · issue 003 · covering roughly the trailing 90 days, weighted to the last two weeks · automated sweep, human judgment applied

Verdict

A pattern emerged — but it is the opposite shape of the one this scan was built to look for.

The broad "every wealthy cohort is heading for the exits" thesis carried by the last run weakened materially this week: Berkshire Hathaway reversed 14 consecutive quarters of net selling and bought roughly $20B of equities. Meanwhile the sweep turned up something more concrete than a mood — a documented, record-setting instance of informed pre-positioning in the three days before a $45B fund's forced liquidation became public. That is not foreknowledge of a future event. It is proof that the channel exists and works.

The trade worked. The people on the other side were whoever was still holding.

Flagged signals

1. Record hedge fund tech selling in the three days before the Situational Awareness liquidation was publicHighest anomaly

Goldman Sachs Prime reported that hedge funds sold more global information-technology equities in the three days ending July 28 than in any comparable period since the dataset began in 2016. The forced liquidation was announced July 30. Reporting characterizes the selling as funds "not waiting for the margin call" but front-running it.

Why it's unusual: this is the rare case where the scan can point at a specific, dated, record-magnitude repositioning that preceded a specific, dated public event — rather than inferring intent from a mood. It is also the rare case with a nameable mechanism. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Bank of America were simultaneously (a) Situational Awareness's prime brokers, working its margin calls, and (b) the prime brokers whose other clients were executing the record sale. No secret cabal is required for that information to move — only a desk, a phone, and a structural conflict that the prime brokerage model creates by design. This is the most investigable item this scan has surfaced, and it is not currently being framed as an information-integrity story anywhere in mainstream coverage.

2. Central banks bought a record 289 tonnes of gold into the worst quarterly price decline since 2013Highest anomaly

Q2 2026 net official-sector purchases reached 288.9 tonnes, up 62% year on year and more than five times the revised Q1 figure of 57 tonnes. Poland's central bank was the largest buyer at 51 tonnes, taking reserves to 632 tonnes; the People's Bank of China added 33 tonnes, its biggest quarterly addition since Q4 2023, to reach 2,346 tonnes. Gold fell roughly 16% over the same quarter.

Why it's unusual: carried forward from the prior issue and still the strangest number in the sweep. Buyers who accelerate into a 16% drawdown are not trading — they are converting. Price-insensitive accumulation by sovereigns is the behavior of a buyer optimizing for custody rather than return. The ordinary explanation is real and sufficient: reserve managers have been de-dollarizing since Russia's reserves were frozen in 2022. But the ordinary explanation does not account for the acceleration into the drawdown.

3. Record leverage sitting in the market's plumbingStrong

Hedge funds now own a record-high 8% of US Treasuries. Combined repo and prime-brokerage borrowing exceeds $6 trillion. Long Treasury exposure has expanded from roughly $600B in 2014 to $2.4T at the end of 2025. The Dallas Fed has published on how rising hedge fund leverage now affects monetary policy implementation itself; Apollo has flagged Treasury-basis leverage at record highs.

Why it's unusual: Situational Awareness was a $45B fund at 4× leverage and its unwind was absorbed in 24 hours by a single buyer. The basis trade is a different order of magnitude in a market where the Fed is the only backstop. The signal is not that anyone knows something — it is that the system's capacity to absorb a forced seller has been tested once this month and the next test is larger.

4. The AI credit market still disagrees with the AI equity marketStrong

Five-year CDS spreads: Oracle near 196.6bp, Meta near 93bp, Nvidia widened to 57.25bp from roughly 42bp in late June. Spreads on Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Oracle, SpaceX and Broadcom have all risen sharply to record levels. Hyperscalers are set to spend over $750B on AI data center infrastructure in 2026, potentially $870B in 2027 — a figure expected to exceed even the largest hyperscalers' free cash flow, leaving debt as the funding route. The BIS has published on the on- and off-balance-sheet borrowing structures underneath it.

Why it's unusual: credit and equity are pricing the same issuers with opposite conviction, and credit is historically the better-informed of the two. July's AI drawdown — the one that killed Situational Awareness — was the equity market briefly agreeing with the credit market, then changing its mind within a fortnight.

5. Prediction markets are functioning as a working insider-trading venueStrong

A New York Times investigation identified more than 80 Polymarket accounts placing suspiciously timed bets, including positions opened in the hours before undisclosed US and Israeli military operations against Iran. In April, US Army Master Sergeant Gannon Ken Van Dyke was federally indicted for allegedly using classified information about Operation Absolute Resolve to net over $409,000. Polymarket has referred nearly 100 wallets to law enforcement. Kalshi flagged a White House teleprompter operator to the CFTC. House Oversight Chair Comer opened a formal probe of both platforms on May 22.

Why it's unusual: this is the scan's clearest evidence that people with genuine advance knowledge of state action monetize it — and it is now documented by indictment rather than inference. It is a proof of concept for the entire premise of this dashboard. Note the direction it points, though: the confirmed cases are mid-level government employees making six figures, not billionaires making ten. The leak channel is real and it is downstream, not upstream.

6. Family offices still rewriting strategic allocation at roughly twice the normal rateStrong

60% of family offices told UBS they plan to change strategic asset allocation within twelve months, the highest reading UBS has recorded and up from 35% a year earlier. De-risking and improving liquidity are the stated primary objectives, with many expecting cash to be the best-returning asset of the coming year. Among offices making active changes, private equity is trending from 21% toward 18% and real estate from roughly 11% to 8%, rotating into private credit, hedge funds and secondaries — shorter duration, more predictable cash flows.

Why it's unusual: strategic asset allocation is by definition the slow-moving layer. A near-doubling in one year of the share of families rewriting it is a tactical move wearing a strategic label. Carried forward from the prior issue; unchanged this week.

7. Executive protection spending keeps climbing — against the public, not against an eventWatch

Threats targeting AI leaders and data centers jumped sevenfold between late February and May. Days after an attempted firebombing at OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's home, an intruder entered Anthropic's lobby to warn that an executive was "going to be killed." Palantir's executive protection budget rose 150% to nearly $3M; Oracle's climbed 85% to $5.6M; Meta spent $27M on Zuckerberg and his family; Nvidia dedicated its entire $4M security budget to Jensen Huang. The share of tech companies disclosing executive protection spending grew from 26.8% in 2021 to 38.1% in 2025.

Why it's unusual — and why it is not a foreknowledge signal: this is real, well-sourced, and accelerating, but it reads as elites hardening against domestic anger about AI and inequality, which is already fully public. It belongs in the panic meter as a cohesion indicator, not in the signal list as evidence anyone was told something. Included here explicitly so the distinction is on the record.

8. Counter-signal: Berkshire Hathaway flipped to net buyer after 14 quarters of sellingContradicts the thesis

Berkshire's cash fell to $365.5B at the end of June from a record $397.4B three months earlier. The company was a net buyer of equities in Q2 with nearly $20B of net purchases, after 14 consecutive quarters as a net seller. It repurchased roughly $4.5B of its own shares, against $235M in Q1 — a nineteen-fold acceleration. Alphabet surged to the fifth-largest position.

Why this matters more than the bullish signals it displaces: the prior issue of this dashboard led with Berkshire's record cash pile as evidence that the best-informed patient capital in the world was refusing to deploy. That evidence has now reversed, and intellectual honesty requires leading with the reversal rather than burying it. A firm that spent three and a half years declining to buy has started buying — including buying its own stock aggressively — which is the single most direct available refutation of "insiders think a crash is imminent." Any scan that only ever accumulates alarming data points is measuring its own method, not the world.

Assessment

The honest read. This run cuts against the last one, and that is the most useful thing it produced. A scan that only ever finds more alarming evidence is measuring its own method rather than the world. Berkshire buying, a market at records, Iran de-escalating and a Bezos sale that turns out to be a scheduled plan are all evidence against the elite-exodus thesis, and they are reported here first.

What survives is narrower and harder. One documented case of record-magnitude informed pre-positioning, with a named mechanism and identifiable institutions on both sides of it. One category of sovereign behavior that remains genuinely inexplicable on ordinary terms — buying a record quantity of an asset while its price collapses. And a leverage stack that just demonstrated, at $45B scale, exactly how fast a forced unwind now travels.

On the central question. No evidence was found that anyone holds knowledge of a specific undisclosed future event. The Situational Awareness front-run is the opposite: knowledge of a specific, imminent, already-determined event, held for days by people positioned to trade it. That is a securities-and-market-integrity story, and a good one. It is not a prophecy. The recurring failure mode of this kind of scan is collapsing shared risk perception into shared knowledge, and the second-most common is treating a confirmed small leak as proof of an unconfirmed large one.

What to check next run. Whether Goldman prime data shows the same pattern ahead of the next forced unwind — a repeat would move this from an anomaly to a mechanism. Whether the Comer probe produces documents. Whether Q3 central bank gold purchases stay elevated with prices still falling. Whether Berkshire's buying continues or was a one-quarter deployment of a specific opportunity.

Method & limits

  • Sources are public web search results as of 2026-08-10. Nothing here comes from private forums, paid data feeds, or non-public filings.   • Secondary reporting was preferred where it cited a named primary source (Goldman Prime, World Gold Council, UBS, J.P. Morgan, CBO/GAO, House Oversight). Figures were not independently re-derived from primary filings.
  • Panic meter scores are editorial judgments on a 0–100 scale anchored to the cited data. The indicator set changed from the prior issue — "information integrity" and "fiscal strain" were added, "institutional trust," "asset prices vs. real economy" and "household solvency" were not re-scored. The 73 → 74 move is therefore not a meaningful trend and should not be read as one. Components carry the argument; the composite is shorthand.
  • Absence of a signal category in this page means the sweep found nothing credible, not that nothing exists.
