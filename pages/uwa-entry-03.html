02 · Flagged signals

Ordered by strength of evidence

Insiders are selling into the record at eleven times the rate they are buying [SEC filings]

US corporate insiders sold $77.6B of stock in the first half of 2026, up 20% year over year and second only to the stimulus-flooded first half of 2021. Purchases over the same period totalled $6.9B. A 3 August filing shows Jeff Bezos selling roughly 15 million shares — about $4B, the largest single insider transaction in five years. Nvidia and CoreWeave insiders have been steady sellers through the AI rally.

Sold H1: $77.6B · Bought H1: $6.9B · Ratio 11.2 : 1 · +20% YoY · Bezos filing: 3 Aug 2026

Why it is unusual: insider selling always runs above buying, so the raw direction means nothing. The signal is the ratio and the timing — near-peak-2021 intensity while the S&P sets record closes and 85% of Q2 reporters beat estimates. People with the clearest view of their own order books are choosing cash over their own equity at a moment the tape says everything is fine. Note the honest counterweight: much of this flows through pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans, which blunts the "they know something" reading.

Sources: Bloomberg, 17 Jul · Yahoo Finance · Finbold on the 3 Aug filing · Crain Currency

The exit door on private credit is being tested — and it does not open all the way [Fund disclosures]

Blackstone gated BCRED — its $79B flagship non-traded BDC — for the first time in June 2026 after redemption requests hit 10% of shares against a 5% quarterly cap. Blue Owl's $36B OCIC faced requests for 21.9% of shares in Q1. Carlyle's CTAC saw 15.7%, over three times its limit. Proskauer's default index rose to 2.73% in Q1 from 1.84% two quarters earlier. The FSB and the ECB both published warnings on private-credit vulnerabilities and bank interlinkage; Jamie Dimon publicly downplayed the sector on the 14 April earnings call.

BCRED gated: 4 Jun 2026 · Requests 10% vs 5% cap · OCIC: 21.9% requested · CTAC: 15.7% requested · Default rate 1.84% → 2.73%

Why it is unusual: the people redeeming from semi-liquid credit vehicles are wealthy allocators and their advisers — the exact cohort this dashboard tracks — and they are trying to leave simultaneously, in size, before any visible crisis. Gating is the mechanism by which a slow-moving worry becomes a fast one, because a gate is public information that rewards whoever queued first. Regulators are on record; the largest bank CEO is on record telling everyone to relax. That divergence is itself the tell.

Sources: CNBC, 4 Jun · Yahoo Finance / FT · Financial Stability Board, May · ECB Financial Stability Review · Forbes

Central banks bought a record tonnage of gold while the price was falling [WGC data]

Official-sector buyers took 289 tonnes in Q2 2026 — a quarterly record — during gold's steepest quarterly decline in ten years, following 244 tonnes in Q1. The World Gold Council's June survey found 45% of central banks intend to add gold over the next twelve months, up from 29% two years ago, and 74% expect the dollar's reserve share to fall over five years. Repatriation of physical bullion out of foreign vaults is running alongside it. Asian and Gulf central banks dominate the flow.

Q2 2026: 289t (record) · Q1 2026: 244t · ~1,000t/yr for 4 years · 45% plan to add (was 29%) · 74% expect USD share to fall

Why it is unusual: buying more as the price falls is the signature of a mandate rather than a trade. A reserve manager chasing returns waits for the dip to stop; a reserve manager hedging the risk that dollar assets can be frozen buys regardless of the quote. The stated trigger is still the 2022 freeze of Russia's reserves. This is sovereign-level insurance against a monetary event, not a view on inflation — and it accelerated this quarter rather than plateauing.

Sources: Q2 record via WGC data · Investing News on repatriation · US Gold Bureau survey summary

Elite relocation hits an all-time high and US net migration turns negative [Industry data]

142,000 millionaires changed country in 2025 — the highest ever recorded — with 165,000 projected for 2026. American net migration has turned negative for the first time in at least half a century, and roughly 5,000 Americans renounced citizenship in 2025. Residency-by-investment firms report enquiry volumes roughly doubling at their US roadshows. The UAE is the largest net gainer; the UK the largest net loser. Singapore, Switzerland, Italy, Greece, Hong Kong and New Zealand absorb most of the rest. Meanwhile a 5% one-time California billionaire wealth tax is on the 2026 ballot.

2025: 142,000 moved · 2026e: 165,000 · US net migration < 0 · ~5,000 renunciations · UAE +12,000 · UK −16,500

Why it is unusual: wealthy people have always bought second passports; the change is that the stated driver has shifted from tax and lifestyle to political and systemic risk, and that the US has flipped from a destination to a source. Advisers describe golden visas as a "pre-exit strategy" — optionality purchased before it is needed. This is the slowest-moving signal on the page and the hardest to fake, because it involves families physically moving.

Sources: Yahoo Finance · Get Golden Visa, 17 Jun · Moneywise on the $40B migration industry

Family offices post the largest reallocation intent in survey history — geopolitics now the top risk [UBS survey]

UBS's 2026 Global Family Office Report surveyed 307 offices averaging $2.7B net worth. Sixty per cent — the largest share the survey has recorded — plan to change asset allocation in the next twelve months. Geopolitical conflict is now the top-ranked risk over both short and long horizons, ahead of markets, with global debt levels and recession rising behind it. Direct investments fell and dollar exposure is being questioned. Separately, BofA's Bull & Bear indicator hit 9.7 and fired its first equity sell signal since 2021, with fund-manager cash at 3.6% — below the 4.0% rule threshold, meaning almost no dry powder.

307 offices · avg $2.7B · 60% reallocating — record · #1 risk: geopolitical conflict · BofA B&B 9.7 → sell signal · FMS cash 3.6%

Why it is unusual: two opposite readings collide here. Family offices — patient, multi-generational, slow to move — are repositioning in record numbers and naming war and state failure rather than valuation as the thing they fear. But institutional cash is simultaneously at a level that says nobody is actually holding anything back. Intent to de-risk plus no dry powder is the fragile combination: the reallocation still has to happen, and it will have to happen through a market with nothing on the other side of it.

Sources: UBS Global Family Office Report 2026, 28 May · Modus analysis · Benzinga on the BofA sell signal

Banks are quietly buying crash protection they cannot easily be seen buying [Trade-desk reporting]

There is a reported surge in over-the-counter "crash puts" — exotic deep-out-of-the-money protection banks use to offload the tail risk they take on from leveraged-ETF issuance. Because these are unlisted OTC contracts, the volume is unquantifiable from public data; the evidence is desk-level reporting rather than exchange prints. Separately, the BIS used its 2026 Annual Report to name AI-capex circular financing as one of the three largest risks to global financial stability, and more than $14B of Oracle-linked data-centre debt has already been repriced.

OTC — volume unquantifiable · BIS top-3 stability risk · $14B+ Oracle debt repriced · CoreWeave facility ~11% coupon

Why it is unusual: the mechanism is what matters. Structural demand for leveraged AI exposure forces dealers to warehouse tail risk, and dealers are now paying up to shed it in a venue with no public tape. That is protection bought where it will not show up in a put/call ratio or a VIX print — which is precisely why the visible fear gauges can look calm while the actual hedging bill rises. Treat the size as unverified; treat the direction as corroborated by a central bank naming the same exposure.

Sources: Bloomberg on crash puts · Capacity on BIS and circular financing · Forbes, 23 Jul · Quinn Emanuel client alert

Ancient bitcoin wallets keep waking up — and moving to cold storage, not exchanges [On-chain, ambiguous]

A wallet dormant seven months moved 16,400 BTC (~$1.04B) on 3 August. A wallet dormant 8.5 years moved 5,908 BTC (~$383M) on 15 July. A 2013-era wallet moved 500 BTC on 4 August. In each case the destination was an unknown address rather than a known exchange deposit, which reads as custody migration rather than distribution. Bitcoin was near $62,800, roughly 50% below its October 2025 peak.

3 Aug: 16,400 BTC · $1.04B · 15 Jul: 5,908 BTC · $383M · 4 Aug: 500 BTC (2013 wallet) · BTC ~$62.8k · −50% from peak

Why it is only a weak signal: a decade-dormant wallet moving is dramatic and almost always mundane — heirs, custodians upgrading key management, an exchange reshuffling reserves. The clustering is mildly interesting; the destinations argue against panic selling. Logged for continuity, not escalated. If any of these coins subsequently land on exchange deposit addresses, that changes the reading.

Sources: CoinDesk, 4 Aug · Cryptonomist, 3 Aug · Yahoo Finance

06 · Assessment and method

Read this before acting on any of it

The pattern is real; the secret is not. Six independent streams — insider sales, private-credit redemptions, official gold buying, family-office reallocation, elite relocation, and OTC tail hedging — all point the same direction: converting claims on the system into assets and jurisdictions that survive the system being wrong. That convergence is genuinely unusual and worth flagging. What is absent is any evidence of coordination or of a specific known event. No dated rumour survived checking, no cryptic elite statement corroborated, and the physical-risk indicators that would accompany real foreknowledge — evacuations, security repositioning, health surveillance — are all quiet.

The most defensible reading is that a large number of wealthy actors independently reached the same conclusion about the same visible risks: an AI capital cycle financed circularly, a private-credit market whose liquidity promises exceed its assets, and a reserve currency whose institutional guardrails were publicly tested this year. Convergent inference looks identical to conspiracy from the outside, and is far more common.

Method and limits. Open-web search across financial press, regulator publications, industry surveys and niche blogs. Everything here is public. Sources are graded — SEC filings and regulator documents at the top, desk-level reporting and single-outlet claims below, aggregator and SEO-farm content excluded from the flagged set. Private forums, paywalled research and non-public communications were not accessible to this run and are a real blind spot. The panic meter is a subjective composite, not a measurement; it exists to make the shape of the week comparable across entries, and its absolute value means nothing.

Not investment advice. Nothing here is a recommendation, and several of the strongest-looking signals have mundane explanations that are stated alongside them on purpose.
