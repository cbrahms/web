Unusual Wealth Activity — Issue 001

Insiders are selling into the loudest bullish tape on record

A scan of filings, allocator surveys, prediction-market enforcement and open-source reporting for evidence that people with better information than the public are repositioning. One coherent pattern emerged, and it is not a secret — it is a divergence hiding in plain sight.

Compiled 7 Aug 2026 · Window Jan–Aug 2026 · Pattern Confirmed · Panic index 68 / 100

Topline — seven topics

  1. Insider distribution at a 20-year extreme. $77.6B of corporate insider selling in H1 2026 at an 11-to-1 sell/buy ratio — while August alone set a 10-year record.
  2. The counterparty is euphoria. S&P 500 call volume hit an all-time 4.02M contracts on 6 Aug; hedge funds made their second-largest weekly equity purchase since 2008.
  3. Family offices are rebuilding portfolios at a record rate. 60% plan to change strategic allocation in the next 12 months — the highest UBS has ever recorded, up from 35%.
  4. Central banks bought gold into a crash. A record 289 tonnes in Q2 2026 during the steepest quarterly price decline in a decade — price-insensitive buying is strategic, not tactical.
  5. Documented foreknowledge trading exists and is being prosecuted. ~$200M in Polymarket trades flagged, ~100 wallets referred to law enforcement, a DOJ case against a servicemember trading on classified information.
  6. Physical hedging is scaling from anecdote to product. 617 wealthy American golden-visa applicants to New Zealand, a 50-city commercial bunker network launching this summer, and executive-protection budgets up across the S&P.
  7. The hardest under-priced fact of the week. The US has expended roughly 80% of its THAAD interceptors and half its Patriots; restocking takes about three years. Markets price this at zero.

Assessment — what the pattern actually is, and what it isn't

The honest read is not that a cabal knows a date. It is that the best-informed holders of financial assets — corporate officers, family offices with a $2.7B average net worth, and sovereign reserve managers — are all reducing exposure to the same thing at the same time, using different instruments, while the marginal buyer is a leveraged call option.

Each individual signal below has a boring explanation. Insiders sell into strength; Berkshire is disciplined about price; central banks diversify after Russia's reserves were frozen. What is not boring is the simultaneity, and the fact that the stated reason across the allocator surveys has shifted from valuation to geopolitics. UBS reports that major geopolitical conflict is now the top family-office concern over both one- and five-year horizons — that is a different worry than "stocks are expensive."

Read it as positioning for a disorderly repricing, not as a countdown. The three candidate triggers that the smart-money conversation keeps circling — AI credit, reserve-currency erosion, and a munitions-depleted US facing a near-peer test — are all discussed openly in trade press and central-bank research, and all under-covered in general news.

Signals — flagged items, ranked by evidentiary weight

SIG-01 · Corporate insiders sold $77.6B in H1 at an 11-to-1 ratio — and August set a 10-year record [Hard data]

  • What: US insider sales reached $77.6B in H1 2026, up ~20% year over year and the second-highest pace in more than 20 years, behind only the 2021 stimulus peak. Purchases were $6.9B — eleven sellers for every buyer. August's $10.3B is a ten-year monthly record.
  • Why unusual: The sell/buy ratio, not the dollar figure, is the signal. Sales can be mechanical; the near-total absence of buying is a choice. Officers with the clearest view of their own order books are declining to add at these prices.
  • Conventional: Most sales run through pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans, and executives holding concentrated post-run-up equity have legitimate diversification reasons. This alone would not be a story.
  • Sources: Bloomberg · CBS News · Finbold

SIG-02 · Their counterparty is the most bullish options tape ever printed [Hard data]

  • What: S&P 500 call volume topped 4,023,796 contracts on 6 Aug 2026 — an all-time high, described by floor traders as "one-way flow." One-month calls paying off on a 10% rally were bid more heavily than puts paying off on a 10% drop, the widest skew since March. Hedge funds bought a net $4.8B of US equities in the week to 31 July, the second-largest weekly purchase since 2008.
  • Why unusual: Divergence is the whole finding. Record insider distribution and record leveraged call demand are the two sides of the same trades. Historically, that configuration resolves toward the informed side.
  • Conventional: Momentum markets produce record call volume routinely near highs, and the index has been range-bound rather than parabolic. Positioning extremes are notoriously bad at timing.
  • Sources: Benzinga / State Street · Schaeffer's

SIG-03 · Family offices are rewriting allocations at the highest rate UBS has ever measured [Hard data]

  • What: Across 307 family offices averaging $2.7B net worth, 60% plan to change strategic asset allocation in the next twelve months — up from 35% a year earlier and the highest reading in the survey's history. Among those changing, real estate falls from 11% to 8% and gold rises from 2% to 3%. Major geopolitical conflict is the top-ranked concern on both the one- and five-year horizon.
  • Why unusual: Strategic allocation is the slowest-moving decision a wealthy family makes; it is meant to survive cycles. A near-doubling in one year of families tearing it up is the closest thing to a coordinated behavior change in this dataset — and unlike insider sales, it cannot be explained by tax or liquidity. Cutting real estate by a quarter while adding gold is a specific bet on illiquidity being punished.
  • Conventional: Survey intent is not action, and the same cohort still names AI its leading thematic exposure at 65% — this is repositioning within risk, not exit from it.
  • Sources: UBS Global Family Office Report 2026 · InvestmentNews · Investor Strategy News

SIG-04 · A live, prosecuted market in foreknowledge — roughly $200M of it [Hard data]

  • What: Polymarket flagged ~$200M of H1 2026 volume as bearing insider-like characteristics — fresh wallets, outsized sizing, entry at long odds, single-market concentration — and referred nearly 100 wallets to law enforcement. Documented cases include entry into a US–Iran peace-deal market at 6% implied odds, and three wallets betting on Maduro's removal hours before an alleged US operation on 4 Jan 2026. In April, DOJ and CFTC charged a US Army servicemember with trading on classified information.
  • Why unusual: This is the only item here that is direct rather than inferential. It establishes that people with non-public knowledge of major geopolitical events are monetizing it in venues we can watch, at scale, and that at least one such person held a security clearance. It also gives a concrete surveillance target for future runs of this scan.
  • Conventional: Flags are not findings. Skilled forecasters produce statistically identical footprints, and both platforms have commercial reasons to publicize enforcement.
  • Sources: Bloomberg Businessweek · crypto.news · CoinDesk · Polymarket Integrity

SIG-05 · US interceptor stocks are depleted, and no asset price reflects it [Hard data]

  • What: Roughly 80% of THAAD interceptors and about half of Patriot inventory were expended in the Iran war. Fewer than 278 THAAD and 827 Patriot interceptors remain, against pre-war estimates of ~452 and ~2,200. Officials say remaining stocks are insufficient to confront a near-peer adversary; replenishment is estimated at around three years.
  • Why unusual: This is the largest gap in the scan between what is verifiably known and what is being priced. It is a dated, bounded vulnerability window that any competent adversary's planning staff has also read — and it lands in the same period family offices name geopolitical conflict their top concern. Defense primes and energy risk premia do not appear to reflect it.
  • Conventional: Stockpile depletion is a procurement problem, not a war signal; US intelligence concurrently lowered its assessment of Chinese invasion intent, judging Beijing has no fixed unification timeline.
  • Sources: CNN, 4 Aug 2026 · Defense News · Task & Purpose · CNN (counter-evidence)

SIG-06 · Central banks bought a record 289 tonnes of gold into the worst quarter in a decade [Corroborated]

  • What: Q2 2026 central-bank gold purchases hit a record 289 tonnes while the gold price posted its steepest quarterly decline in ten years, following 244 tonnes in Q1. Four-year average net purchases run near 1,000 tonnes annually. 45% of surveyed central banks intend to add gold over the next twelve months; 74% expect the dollar's reserve share to decline over five years.
  • Why unusual: Buying more as price falls hardest is the tell. Return-seeking capital does not behave this way; reserve managers executing a policy mandate do. The stated driver — that reserves held in someone else's currency can be frozen, as Russia's ~$300B were — is a sovereign-risk hedge, not an inflation trade.
  • Conventional: This trend is four years old and well documented; it is a slow structural rotation, not a new development. Note also that much of the available reporting on it comes from bullion dealers with a direct commercial interest — the tonnage figures trace to World Gold Council data, but the framing in those outlets is promotional.
  • Sources: GoldSilver (dealer — figures via WGC) · Crypto Briefing · Discovery Alert

SIG-07 · AI credit went from a worry to the consensus top systemic risk in about a month [Hard data]

  • What: 48% of BofA-surveyed fund managers name AI data-center debt the top systemic credit risk of 2026; those calling the AI bubble the single biggest tail risk rose from 28% in June to 45%. Private bond issuance hit ~$81B through May, the strongest start on record since 2016, with S&P projecting growth from $56.6B in 2026 to $215B by 2028. The BIS has designated the buildout systemically risky, citing circular financing and off-balance-sheet structures. Senators pressed FSOC in January to open a formal probe.
  • Why unusual: A 17-point jump in one month in what professional allocators fear most is fast for a survey that usually crawls. Note the shape: the debt is private, so the mark-to-market that would normally warn the public does not exist. First defaults are projected for 2027–28 as initial leases renew.
  • Conventional: Fund-manager surveys track sentiment, and the same managers remain heavily long the trade. Bubble-calling has been continuous since 2023 and has been wrong throughout.
  • Sources: BofA survey coverage · BIS study coverage · Oliver Wyman · US Senate Banking (minority)

SIG-08 · Berkshire's $397B is the largest single dry-powder position in corporate history [Corroborated]

  • What: Berkshire held a record $397.4B in cash and equivalents at 31 Mar 2026, past the ~$373B end-2025 high, after selling $24.1B of equities against $15.9B bought — a net-selling streak now past three years. The Apple position has fallen from a peak above 915M shares to 228M.
  • Why unusual: Twelve consecutive quarters of net selling is a stance, not a trade. It corroborates SIG-01 from an entirely different actor type: the most price-disciplined buyer in public markets cannot find anything to buy.
  • Conventional: This is the most over-read datapoint in finance. Berkshire's own framing is that it reflects a shortage of adequately priced opportunities and a leadership transition building optionality — not a crash forecast. Treat as texture, not evidence.
  • Sources: Motley Fool · TheStreet

SIG-09 · Physical hedging has industrialized: visas, bunkers, bodyguards [Corroborated]

  • What: New Zealand's Active Investor Plus golden visa has drawn 1,833 applicants since the April 2025 loosening, of whom 617 — roughly 40% — are wealthy Americans, covering 766 people from 252 US applications between April 2025 and June 2026 at a NZ$5M minimum. Separately, the Aerie bunker network opens its first site in summer 2026 with a stated plan of 50 US cities and 1,000 affiliate locations. Executive protection continues to scale: Apple disclosed $887,870 for Tim Cook, Microsoft a 45% jump for Satya Nadella, Meta ~$27M for Mark Zuckerberg, and 27% of surveyed firms now provide CEO personal security, up 59% in two years.
  • Why unusual: The shift is from bespoke to commercial. A single billionaire building a bunker is an eccentricity; a franchised network targeting 50 cities means somebody underwrote sustained demand. Applicants reportedly cite domestic political conditions rather than tax.
  • Conventional: Golden-visa volume responds to the rule change that created it, and security spending traces to a specific 2024 assassination rather than to any forecast. The bunker figure is a developer's marketing projection, not built capacity.
  • Sources: Taipei Times · Fortune · Forbes · Axios

SIG-10 · Watchlist: "harvest now, decrypt later" is a policy deadline, not a theory [Thin / watch]

  • What: The European Commission requires national post-quantum transition roadmaps by December 2026, and Forrester's 2026 assessment treats Q-Day as plausible by 2030. Adversaries are described as already archiving encrypted traffic for later decryption.
  • Why watching: Included as a negative result worth tracking. This is a topic where institutions are acting on a timeline the public conversation has not absorbed — the structural shape of a real information asymmetry — but there is no wealth-flow evidence attached to it yet. Nothing in the filings, survey or flow data this cycle connects to it.
  • Sources: FedTech · Quantum Zeitgeist

Method — what was excluded, and the limits of this brief

  • Promotional doom content. Substack and YouTube items packaging the insider-selling data with "depopulation event" framing were dropped; the underlying figures were re-sourced to Bloomberg and CBS before use.
  • Commercially conflicted sources. Most available de-dollarization coverage originates with bullion dealers selling the recommended hedge. Tonnage figures were retained because they trace to World Gold Council data; the framing was discarded, and SIG-06 is labeled accordingly.
  • Single-actor price manipulation. Statements by political figures or executives that move a specific asset were excluded by design. Nothing in this brief rests on one person's remarks.
  • Cryptic-utterance searches returned nothing. Direct searches for "something big is coming," "on good authority" and similar phrasings surfaced no credible instance from a wealthy or institutional source. The best-evidenced foreknowledge in this cycle (SIG-04) left a transaction record, not a quote — which is the more reliable place to look.
  • What would change the assessment. Insider sell/buy ratio normalizing below ~5:1; family-office allocation intent reverting toward 35%; interceptor production contracts accelerating; or put skew inverting against the current call bid. Each is checkable on the next run.
  • Standing caveat. Every item here is public information. The pattern is inference from convergent positioning, not evidence of coordination or of anyone possessing knowledge of a specific future event. Read as risk assessment, not prophecy.
